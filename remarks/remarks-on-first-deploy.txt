\- separate grocery from small market, they are fundamentally different from each other. 

\- for now, let's start with small options that we will test on as we go, and make more business options. Criticize this point if you disagree.

\- the handle field is ambiguous, it doesn't say exactly what it's for. it should contain more info. "Its unique name in the app” means nothing to a café owner. the handle goes into the shop page address, invite links, and the team’s log-in (ahmed@nova). Show that as they type:

Your shop page: <domain>/tree-smouha
Your team logs in as: moe@tree-smouha

That one preview explains the field and leads people to pick a good handle.

\- arabic names gives an empty box, this should be accounted for. handleFrom removes everything that isn't latin. A big margin of our target users will type in arabic, so supporting it is essential.

\- Under the head "Set up your business", a quote stating "You can change all of it later" is there, this isn't specifically true for the handle. Once a cafe prints a QR code or shares it's telegram link, changing the handle breaks everything. If we want to make this statement true, having the business handle be changed would require us to set up everything else, QR code, and telegram bot/link.

\- Taking someone else’s name. The reserved list only covers system words. Anyone can register cilantro, costa, seoudi, carrefour or starbucks. Add:

\- a list of known Egyptian chains and brands, plus a report-and-reclaim process;
\- your own brand name once you choose it;
\- paths you’ll need later (menu, order, orders, pay, checkout, invite, join, careers, features, faq, contact, legal, sw, manifest, oauth, telegram, whatsapp, verify, official, ...).

\- The deeper issue: <domain>/<shop> shares one namespace with your own site. Every page the landing site adds in the future can clash with a business that already has that handle. <shop>.<domain>, or a prefix like <domain>/s/<shop>, removes the problem for good. It’s cheap to decide now and expensive later (design section 20).

\- "One name for the whole business. Branches come later" if we are accounting business that have more than one branch, this should be added.

Bugs and behavior problems:
\- After “Build my business”, the owner lands on My Account, not Home. In App.tsx:78 and App.tsx:59, the first thing a new owner sees is their devices and password settings. The design’s first-time Home (“Welcome, Ahmed. Nova Café is ready. Let’s add what you sell first”) is where they should go. Home isn’t built yet, but this target should be noted in FE-004.

\- A retry can create a second business. POST /api/app/businesses has no retry key. If the request succeeds but the reply is lost (common on mobile data), the retry gets handle_taken for their own business. The person then picks a new handle and ends up with two businesses. This breaks design rule 2.1 (“idempotency keys on anything that can be retried”). Fix: accept an idempotency_key, or on handle_taken check whether that handle is already theirs and treat it as success.

\- The 5-business limit shows the wrong message. service.ts:103 throws limit_reached with status 429, which the app shows as “Too many tries. Please wait a little.” Waiting will never help. Give it its own code, for example business_limit: “One account can have up to 5 businesses.”

\- Invited staff will be forced to create a business. The rule “no business, so /setup, and only Log out gets you out” (App.tsx:56) traps a barista who signs up from an invite link, and anyone removed from their only business. Invites are a term 1 feature, so the rule needs an exception now: an account with a pending invite goes to the invite, not to setup. Design 7.7 should say so too.

\- The handle stops following the name after one edit. If someone edits the handle and then clears it, handleEdited stays true, so the field never follows the business name again. Small, but annoying.

What to remove

\- “Who works in it?” Remove it for now, or give it a real effect (see the next section). A yes/no “team” answer also loses what actually matters later (3 staff or 15).

\- Pharmacy. In Egypt, drug prices are set by the government and prescriptions are regulated. An AI customer agent answering “can I take X with Y?” is a medical and legal risk. Remove it, or block it explicitly until there’s a template with hard rules (no medical advice, hand off at once).

\- “Online shop.” It’s a way of selling, not a kind of business. An online clothes shop is “clothes”. Remove it, or turn it into a later question like “Do you sell online?”

\- “Other services” and “Something else.” Two vague options next to each other confuse people. Merge them into one.

\- The “{name} is ready” screen, as it is now. It’s a click with nothing on it. Either remove it or make it the reward (see below).

What to add

\- Make the done screen the reward. Show their shop page address (this is when the handle starts to mean something), the three first-step buttons from 17.1 (menu photo, add a product, connect Telegram), and a one-line note of the defaults: Arabic · EGP · Cairo time.

\- Language before anything else. The whole flow is English, but the account defaults to ar and the design says Arabic first. Put a language switch at the top of setup, with Arabic as the default. Right now your main users get a setup they may not fully read.

\- “Where is it?” (city and area) instead of “who works in it”. It’s one picker, and it’s useful from the first day: the customer agent can answer “where are you?”, delivery areas start from it, the shop page needs it, and it tells the Market team which neighbourhoods sign up.

\- A short text box after “Something else”: “What do you sell?” It’s free market research and tells you which template to build third. With the term 1 targets (30 to 50 sign-ups), knowing the mix of types is real evidence for the presentation.

\- Optionally, “What should the AI call you?” instead of the plain “Your name”. It gives the field a clear purpose and sets the tone (“Ahmed” or "أستاذ أحمد"). It would only be asked when the account has no name, as today.

\- Funnel numbers. Record which step people stop at. It’s cheap, and it shows whether setup really takes minutes.

The setup I’d aim for

\- Language (Arabic preselected)
\- Business name, with the handle filled in automatically (transliterated) and a live preview of the shop address
\- Kind of business: café and market marked as ready, a few honest others, and “something else” with a text box
\- Area
\- Done: the shop page address and the three first-step buttons, then Home
